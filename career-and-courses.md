## How courses and careers connect

A course is not a career. Most graduates work in jobs that take people from many different courses, and many change fields during their working lives. So it helps to ask two questions about any course you're considering:

- **Is it an "open door"?** Courses such as economics, the sciences, the humanities, business and computing lead to a wide range of jobs. What you do alongside your studies (internships, projects, CCAs, languages) shapes where you go next.
- **Is it a "specific door"?** Some careers can only be entered through a particular degree, usually because you need it to be registered or licensed to practise. If you want one of these careers, the course choice matters a lot more.

## Exploring a field

| Field | Is a specific degree usually needed? |
|---|---|
| Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, nursing | **Yes.** You need the professional degree to register and practise. |
| Law | **Yes.** A recognised law degree, then further training to qualify. |
| Architecture | **Yes.** An accredited architecture degree, then further training. |
| Engineering | **Usually.** Most engineering roles ask for an engineering degree. |
| Accountancy | **Often.** An accountancy degree, or a professional qualification taken after another degree. |
| Psychology | **Yes, to practise.** A psychology degree, usually followed by postgraduate study. |
| Teaching | **No.** A degree in a subject you'd teach, then teacher training. |
| Business, finance, consulting | **No.** Graduates come from many courses. |
| Technology and data | **No, but it helps.** Computing, engineering and maths are common routes; others get in through skills and projects. |
| Public service | **No.** Agencies recruit from most courses. |
| Media, design and the arts | **No.** A portfolio often matters more than the name of your degree. |

Rules for professional careers can change. Check with the professional body or the university before you commit.

**Ways to find out more about a field:**

- **[MySkillsFuture for Students](https://www.myskillsfuture.gov.sg/content/student/en/myskillsfuture-for-students.html)**: explore careers that match your self-assessment results.
- **[Skills Framework](https://www.skillsfuture.gov.sg/skills-framework)**: for each sector of Singapore's economy, the job roles, the skills they need and how people progress.
- **[Careers@Gov](https://www.careers.gov.sg/explore-sectors/)**: what the public service does, sector by sector.
- **Talk to people who do the job.** The [Sec 4 ECG Experience](programmes.md#sec-4-ecg-experience) fireside chats and [HECGS Day](programmes.md#jc1--jc2-hecgs-day) are your chance. Ask what a typical day is like and what they wish they'd known.
- **Try it out.** An [internship](internships.md) or a few days of job shadowing tells you more than any website.

## From JC subjects to courses

Some university courses need particular H2 subjects, for example H2 Mathematics for many engineering and computing courses, and particular sciences for medicine. Check a course's **subject prerequisites** before you rule it in or out: see [A-Level Results & Applying](results-and-applying.md#3-check-subject-prerequisites).

If you're still choosing your JC subjects, look up the prerequisites of two or three courses you might want first. It's easier to keep a door open than to reopen it later.

## Reading graduate employment data

Every year, Singapore's autonomous universities survey their graduates about six months after their final exams. MOE publishes the results as the **[Graduate Employment Survey](https://data.gov.sg/dataset/graduate-employment-survey-ntu-nus-sit-smu-suss-sutd)**: employment rates and starting salaries for each course.

It's useful, but read it carefully:

- **Median, not mean.** The median (the middle graduate) is a fairer picture than the mean, which a few high salaries can pull up.
- **"Employed" includes part-time and temporary work.** Look at the full-time permanent employment rate as well.
- **Look across several years.** Small courses can swing a lot from one year to the next.
- **It's a starting point, not a career.** It says nothing about pay ten years on, job satisfaction, or graduates who go on to further study.

## How to choose a course

Three questions to work through, ideally with a parent, a teacher or a senior:

1. **Who am I?** What do I enjoy? What are my values, interests, personality and strengths? What motivates me?
2. **Where do I want to go?** Which universities and courses fit my goals? How will a course build on my strengths?
3. **How do I get there?** What are the entry requirements? What will it cost, and which scholarships or aid could help?

Go to the universities' open houses before you decide.

## Other routes after A-Levels

University straight after A-Levels isn't the only path.

- **Polytechnic.** A-Level holders can apply directly to each polytechnic for shorter diplomas, often two to two and a half years. See [MOE: Direct Admissions Exercise](https://www.moe.gov.sg/post-secondary/admissions/dae) and each polytechnic's site.
- **The arts.** The [University of the Arts Singapore](singapore-universities.md#university-of-the-arts-singapore) offers degrees through LASALLE and NAFA.
- **Overseas.** See [Overseas Universities](overseas-universities.md).
- **Private universities.** Many run degrees awarded by overseas partner universities. Before you enrol, check that the school is registered under Singapore's Private Education Act, which university awards the degree, and how that degree is recognised. Then check the **3Rs**: your **Reasons** (what will this course let me do, and will employers or professional bodies recognise it?), your **Readiness** (can I manage the workload and the fees?) and the **Risks** (for example, a school closing or limited refunds). See [The 3Rs before you enrol](https://go.gov.sg/3rs). [Ask the ECG team](get-help.md#counselling) if you're unsure.
- **Retaking the A-Levels.** Some students retake the exams as private candidates to improve their grades. Before you decide, ask yourself what you'll do differently this time, and how you'll keep going without school around you. See [SEAB: Private candidates](https://www.seab.gov.sg/private-candidate-start-here/) and [talk to the ECG Counsellor](get-help.md#counselling) first.
- **National Service.** Most boys enlist after their A-Levels. You can apply to the autonomous universities before or during NS, and they may hold your place until you finish. Overseas universities vary. See [Education before and after NS](https://life.gov.sg/guides/parent/education-before-after-ns).
- **Time to work or explore first.** Some students work or intern before university. See [Current Opportunities](opportunities.md) and [Internships](internships.md).
