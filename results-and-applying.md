## 1. Work out your University Admission Score

From AY2026 admission, your University Admission Score is out of **70 points**. It counts:

- your **three H2 content subjects**, and
- **H1 General Paper**.

A **fourth content subject** (H1 or H2) and/or **H1 Mother Tongue** is counted only if it raises your score; the total is then rescaled to 70. **Project Work** is now graded Pass/Fail and does not add points, but you **must pass** it to be admitted to an autonomous university. H2 Knowledge and Inquiry no longer replaces General Paper.

If you sat the A-Levels in 2024 or earlier, your old 90-point score is scaled proportionately to 70. The calculator below doesn't cover this; ask the ECG Counsellor.

### Calculate your score

Enter your grades to see your University Admission Score and how it was worked out. Points per grade: H2 A 20, B 17.5, C 15, D 12.5, E 10, S 5, U 0. H1 subjects score half of that (A 10 down to S 2.5).

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>Worked example (made-up grades)</summary>

A student has these results:

| Subject | Grade | Points |
|---|---|---|
| H2 subject 1 | A | 20 |
| H2 subject 2 | B | 17.5 |
| H2 subject 3 | C | 15 |
| H1 General Paper | B | 8.75 |
| H1 fourth subject | A | 10 |
| H1 Mother Tongue | C | 7.5 |
| Project Work | Pass | — |

The universities work out the score four ways and use the highest:

| Combination | Raw total | Out of 70 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 H2 + GP | 61.25 / 70 | **61.25** |
| 3 H2 + GP + Mother Tongue | 68.75 / 80 | 60.16 |
| 3 H2 + GP + fourth subject | 71.25 / 80 | **62.34** ← used |
| 3 H2 + GP + fourth subject + Mother Tongue | 78.75 / 90 | 61.25 |

Here the H1 A in the fourth subject raises the score, and the Mother Tongue C would lower it, so it's left out. Try the same grades in the calculator above.
</details>

## 2. Read Indicative Grade Profiles (IGPs) correctly

Each autonomous university publishes an **Indicative Grade Profile (IGP)** every year. It shows the grades of applicants offered a place in the **previous** year, at the 10th and 90th percentile, as three H2 grades and one H1 grade.

For example, a 10th-percentile profile of **BCC/B** means 90% of applicants offered a place scored above it. A 90th-percentile profile of **AAA/B** means only 10% scored above it.

{: .important }
> **An IGP is not a cut-off.** It is a reference point. If you have a strong interest in a course and a well-rounded profile, you can still apply even if your grades are below its IGP.

IGPs: [NUS](https://www.nus.edu.sg/oam/admissions/indicative-grade-profile) · [NTU](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/indicative-grade-profile) · [SMU](https://admissions.smu.edu.sg/admissions-requirements/indicative-grade-profile) · [SUTD](https://www.sutd.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/singapore-cambridge-gce-a-level/indicative-grade-profile-igp/) · [SIT](https://singaporetech.edu.sg/files/igp) · [SUSS](https://www.suss.edu.sg/full-time-undergraduate/admissions/indicative-grade-profile-igp)

## 4. Consider Aptitude-Based Admissions (ABA)

Universities look beyond grades. Through **Aptitude-Based Admissions**, they consider your passions, strengths and achievements in areas such as sports, the arts, entrepreneurship and volunteering, using interviews, aptitude tests, essays and portfolios.

What this means for you: be clear about **why** you want a course and how it fits your longer-term goals, and keep a record of what you've done. Each university runs ABA differently:

| University | How it works | Details |
|---|---|---|
| NUS | Short responses and a list of your achievements in the application | [NUS ABA](https://www.nus.edu.sg/oam/admissions/aptitude-based-admissions) |
| NTU | An ABA section with an essay and appraisals | [NTU ABA](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/admission-guide/Aptitude-based-Admissions) |
| SMU | No separate ABA route; every application is assessed holistically | [SMU FAQ](https://admissions.smu.edu.sg/faq/Admissions%20and%20applications) |
| SUTD | Academics, personal qualities and portfolio; some applicants are interviewed | [SUTD criteria](https://www.sutd.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/singapore-cambridge-gce-a-level/criteria-for-admission) |
| SIT | Personal statement and experience alongside grades | [SIT A-Levels](https://www.singaporetech.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/requirements/a-levels) |
| SUSS | Holistic: grades plus essay, assessment and interviews | [SUSS eligibility](https://www.suss.edu.sg/full-time-undergraduate/admissions/eligibility) |

## 5. Apply

Apply directly to each university during its application window. Shortlist courses on [Singapore Universities](singapore-universities.md), then apply through each university's portal: [NUS](https://www.nus.edu.sg/oam/admissions/application-overview) · [NTU](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/admissions/application-process) · [SMU](https://admissions.smu.edu.sg/admissions-requirements/singapore-cambridge-gce-levels) · [SUTD](https://www.sutd.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/application-guide/) · [SIT](https://learner.singaporetech.edu.sg/) · [SUSS](https://www.suss.edu.sg/full-time-undergraduate/how-to-apply)

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>Writing your personal statement</summary>

A personal statement shows your interest in the subject, why you're suited to it, and how your experiences shaped your choice.

- **Before you write:** reflect on your interests, strengths and experiences that relate to the course, and read up on the course and the university.
- **When you write:** be authentic; show rather than tell, with specific examples; be clear and concise; avoid clichés.
- **After you write:** ask a teacher, mentor or the ECG Counsellor for feedback, expect several drafts, and proofread carefully.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>Asking for a recommendation</summary>

- Ask someone who knows you well, such as a teacher who supervised your project.
- Ask politely and early: four to six weeks before the deadline, and earlier still for school internal deadlines.
- Give them your deadline, the format required, and a short bullet list of your achievements, subjects and activities.
- Never list someone as a referee without asking first. Thank them, and tell them the outcome.

Who can write your recommendation letter: see [Who to ask](get-help.md#recommendation-letters). Transcripts and testimonials: see [Who to ask](get-help.md#transcripts).
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>Preparing for an interview</summary>

Interviews may be one-to-one, panel, group or online.

- Reread your application; interviewers often refer to it.
- Prepare two or three questions the website doesn't already answer, for example about research or internship opportunities, or graduates' pathways.
- Practise common questions aloud: *Tell me about yourself. Why this course? How would you contribute? Tell me about a challenge you overcame.*
- Keep up with current affairs related to your field.
- Online: check your connection and keep your background tidy. In person: arrive early.
- Send a short thank-you email afterwards.

The ECG Counsellor runs mock interviews: [book a session](get-help.md#book-a-session).
</details>

## 6. Scholarships and financial aid

Scholarship deadlines fall in the same weeks as university applications. See [Scholarships](scholarships.md) and [Financial Aid](financial-aid.md).

## 7. Talk to ECG

**On results day**, the ECG team gives a short Higher Education and Scholarships briefing, and ECG counselling booths are set up in the hall. Come with your questions: which courses to shortlist, whether to apply for a course above your grades, and which scholarships to consider.

**After results day**, we can:
- advise on choosing courses and universities
- read your personal statement or scholarship essays and give feedback
- coach you for interviews

[Book a session](get-help.md#book-a-session) with the ECG Counsellor, or [send us a question](get-help.md). Let us know when you apply for a scholarship, are called for an interview, or receive an award, so we can support you.
