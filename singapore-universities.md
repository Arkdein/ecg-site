## The six universities at a glance

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>NUS — comprehensive, research-intensive</summary>

Many faculties across three campuses, with interdisciplinary colleges and a strong global offer: NUS Overseas Colleges, student exchange, and double and joint degrees with partner universities. [Aptitude-Based Admissions](https://www.nus.edu.sg/oam/admissions/aptitude-based-admissions)
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>NTU — comprehensive, strong in engineering, science and technology</summary>

An interdisciplinary common core for all undergraduates. Flagship programmes include the Renaissance Engineering Programme and the research-focused CN Yang Scholars Programme. [Aptitude-Based Admissions](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/admissions/undergraduate/admission-guide/Aptitude-based-Admissions)
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>SMU — city campus, seminar-style teaching</summary>

Small seminar classes, a core curriculum, and required global exposure, community service and internships. SMU-X courses have students working on real problems with industry partners. SMU assesses every application holistically rather than through a separate ABA route.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>SUTD — design-centred technology and architecture</summary>

A multidisciplinary, design-focused curriculum in engineering, computing, architecture and design and AI, with humanities and entrepreneurship built in. First-year students learn in small cohort classes.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>SIT — applied degrees with industry</summary>

Practice-oriented degrees, including some with overseas partner universities. The Integrated Work Study Programme places students in relevant work for an extended period during their studies.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>SUSS — applied social sciences</summary>

Applied degrees focused on social and community development. Full-time students learn alongside working adults, with flexible, modular study.
</details>

## University of the Arts Singapore

The **University of the Arts Singapore** is Singapore's arts university, formed by an alliance of LASALLE College of the Arts and the Nanyang Academy of Fine Arts. It is **not** one of the six autonomous universities: you apply **through LASALLE or NAFA**, and there is one intake each August. Eligible Singapore Citizens and PRs on selected programmes receive MOE subsidies and the Tuition Grant.

[UAS admissions](https://uas.edu.sg/admissions) · [Admissions FAQ](https://uas.edu.sg/admissions/faq) · [Apply via LASALLE](https://www.lasalle.edu.sg/admissions) · [Apply via NAFA](https://www.nafa.edu.sg/admissions)

## How to choose a course

Three questions to work through, ideally with a parent, a teacher or a senior:

1. **Who am I?** What do I enjoy? What are my values, interests, personality and strengths? What motivates me?
2. **Where do I want to go?** Which universities and courses fit my goals? How will a course build on my strengths?
3. **How do I get there?** What are the entry requirements? What will it cost, and which scholarships or aid could help?

Go to the universities' open houses before you decide.

### Deciding between options: a weighted table

If you're torn between courses or universities, try this:

1. In the first column, list what matters to you (e.g. career options, overseas exposure, cost, campus life).
2. Across the top, list your options.
3. Rate each option on each consideration from 1 to 10.
4. Give each consideration an importance weight from 1 to 5.
5. Multiply each rating by its weight.
6. Add up each column and compare.

The numbers won't decide for you, but they make the differences between options easier to see.
