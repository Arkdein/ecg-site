# United Kingdom

Is the UK system for me, and how do I apply through UCAS?

UK degrees are recognised worldwide. Most undergraduate degrees take **three years** (four in Scotland), and you study your chosen subject from the first year, so you need to be fairly sure what you want to read. You apply to up to five courses through **UCAS**, the UK's central admissions service.

**In JC2 and applying now?** Check the dates below. **In JC1?** Start with [Is the UK right for you?](#is-the-uk-right-for-you) and [Your UCAS timeline](#your-ucas-timeline).

{: .deadline }
> **2027 entry** ([UCAS dates and deadlines](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/dates-and-deadlines-for-uni-applications)):
> - **15 Oct 2026**, 6pm UK time: Oxford, Cambridge, and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses
> - **13 Jan 2027**, 6pm UK time: equal-consideration deadline for most other courses
>
> RVHS's own deadlines come earlier, so the school has time to check your application and add its reference. Ask for your reference at least four to six weeks before your UCAS deadline. See [Who to ask](get-help.md#uk).

## How UCAS works

{% include steps.html steps="Choose courses|Fill in the form|School reference|UCAS sends it|Universities decide" label="How a UCAS application moves" %}

1. **You choose** an academic direction and up to five exact courses.
2. **You fill in** one online form: your details, qualifications, course choices and three personal statement answers.
3. **RVHS checks** your application, adds the school reference and predicted grades, and sends it to UCAS.
4. **UCAS sends** the same application to every university you chose.
5. **Each university decides.** It may make an offer, invite you to an interview or test, or turn you down.

### The rules that matter most

- **Five choices in total.** They can be at the same or different universities, and for the same or different courses.
- **Four at most for medicine, dentistry, veterinary medicine or veterinary science.** The fifth can be a different subject.
- **Oxford or Cambridge, not both**, in the same year (one course at one of them).
- **One personal statement goes to every choice.** So your five courses need to share a clear academic direction.
- **Your choices aren't ranked**, and universities can't see your other choices while they decide.
- **School and test deadlines come first.** Some courses need an admissions test with its own registration date. Missing the school's deadline or a test registration can end an application.

If you send an early application (for Oxford, Cambridge or medicine) with fewer than five choices, you can add courses with a later deadline afterwards. Check the RVHS procedure with the UK universities teacher first.

Full rules: [UCAS: filling in your application](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/filling-in-your-ucas-application).

### What you're responsible for

The application is yours. You:

- research exact courses and their requirements
- register for any admissions tests and complete any extra forms a university asks for
- give your civics tutor your course list and evidence early, so the reference can support it
- enter every qualification exactly as it appears on your official records
- meet the RVHS deadlines and check your UCAS messages and email

Your civics tutor and subject tutors write the school reference. The RVHS UCAS team links your application to the school, checks it and sends it to UCAS.

## Is the UK right for you?

Choosing a university starts with fit, not a league table. A famous university can't make up for a course you don't want, can't afford or can't get into.

Before a course goes on your shortlist, answer these:

| Question | Where to find the answer |
|---|---|
| Does the exact course suit **what and how** you want to study? | The course page: compulsory modules, options, teaching and assessment |
| Do you meet its **subject, grade and test** requirements? | The university's international (Singapore) entry requirements |
| Can your family or sponsor meet the **full cost**? | Current fees on the university's site, plus a realistic budget for living costs, travel and exchange rates. See [Scholarships](scholarships.md) and [Financial Aid](financial-aid.md) |
| If you have a **sponsor or scholarship**, will it approve this exact course and university? | The sponsor's current rules, or written confirmation from the sponsor |
| If **National Service** applies to you, will the course hold your place while you serve? | The university's deferral policy, or ask its admissions office |
| For a **regulated profession**, will Singapore recognise the degree? | Law: [SILE Qualified Person rules](https://www.sile.edu.sg/qualified-person). Medicine: [SMC registration for international medical graduates](https://www.smc.gov.sg/for-professionals/apply-for-registration/). Others: the professional body |

{: .important }
> If your plan depends on a sponsor, a course that's realistic for you academically still isn't a safe choice unless the sponsor would fund it.

## Choosing your five courses

### Subject first, university second

Start with the subject you want to study, then find the courses that teach it the way you want. Courses with similar names can be very different: Mechanical Engineering, General Engineering, Design Engineering and Sustainable Energy Engineering share an interest but not a curriculum. Record the **exact course title and course code** for every choice.

[Career & Courses](career-and-courses.md#how-to-choose-a-course) has questions to help you decide, and a weighted table for comparing options.

### Keep a record for every course

For each course, note the answer, the link you got it from, and the date you checked:

- **Exact course:** title, code, degree type and start year
- **Requirements:** grades, subjects and English for Singapore applicants
- **Selection:** admissions test, interview, written work or extra form
- **Curriculum and teaching:** compulsory modules, assessment and workload
- **Practical fit:** placements or a year abroad, professional accreditation, cost, deadlines and deferral policy

Check facts in this order: the official course page, the university's international admissions page, UCAS, then the test or accrediting body. Ranking sites are fine for a first look, but not for requirements, fees or deadlines.

### A balanced set of five

A rough guide, not a formula:

| Type of choice | How many | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Highly aspirational | 1–2 | You meet the requirements, but competition, tests or interviews make the outcome hard to predict. |
| Competitive and plausible | 2 | Your grades, subjects and evidence fit the course well. It is still selective. |
| More attainable | 1–2 | You meet the requirements comfortably, and you would genuinely go there. |

Every choice must fit the same academic direction, and you must be able to fund it (with sponsor approval, if you need it).

### Common mistakes

| If you're thinking… | Check this instead |
|---|---|
| "I meet the minimum grades, so it's realistic." | Competition, your subject mix, tests, interviews and the evidence the course values. Minimum grades show you can apply, not that you'll get an offer. |
| "This university ranks highest overall." | The curriculum, teaching, assessment and recognition of the exact course. |
| "All five can be dream choices." | Include different levels of selectivity and at least one credible route to an offer. |
| "My fifth choice is automatically safe." | Would you go there? Can you fund it? Would your sponsor approve it? |
| "These courses are related enough." | Could the same three personal statement answers support every one of them? |
| "Last year's rule probably still applies." | Check the official source again and note the date. Seniors' experiences are useful, but they aren't policy. |

## Your UCAS timeline

Preparation starts well before the UCAS form opens. For the exact dates in your year, see the [UCAS dates](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/dates-and-deadlines-for-uni-applications) and the RVHS deadlines from the UK universities teacher.

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>JC1, October to December: explore subjects</summary>

Read course pages, compare curricula, and note subject prerequisites and any professional recognition you'll need.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>JC2, January to April: build academic evidence</summary>

Go deeper in your subject through reading, projects, competitions, research or relevant experience. Write down what you learnt: it becomes material for your personal statement.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>JC2, May to August: prepare the application</summary>

Shortlist exact courses, talk to your civics tutor, draft your three personal statement answers, and check and register for admissions tests.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>JC2, September to October: early applications</summary>

Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary applicants submit by the RVHS internal deadline, ahead of the 15 October UCAS deadline.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>JC2, November to January: standard applications</summary>

Finish the form, reference and final checks well before the RVHS internal deadline for the January UCAS date.
</details>

<details class="ecg-level" markdown="1">
<summary>The year you start: decisions and entry</summary>

Respond to interview invitations and requests, send your results if asked, compare offers, and choose your firm and insurance choices.
</details>

## The UCAS personal statement

From 2026 entry, the personal statement is **three questions**, with up to **4,000 characters in total** and at least **350 characters per answer**:

1. Why do you want to study this course or subject?
2. How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare for this course or subject?
3. What else have you done to prepare outside of education, and why are these experiences useful?

[UCAS guide to the new personal statement](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/how-to-write-your-personal-statement-for-2026-entry-onwards)
